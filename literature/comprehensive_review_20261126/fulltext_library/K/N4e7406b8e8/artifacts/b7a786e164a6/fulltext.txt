Introduction
Although a significant subset of pediatric acute myeloid 
leukemia (AML) patients are treated with a chemotherapy-
only strategy, allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplan-
tation (HSCT) is a key component of therapy for high-risk 
patients and for those who relapse [1,2]. Past studies have 
shown long-term leukemia-free survival (LFS) of 40%-50% 
for pediatric AML patients who received HSCT in second 
complete remission (CR) [3,4].
The pre-transplant conditioning of pediatric AML patients 
receiving HSCT is commonly myeloablative; however, the 
optimum conditioning regimen remains unclear. Data based 
mostly on adult HSCT patients showed better transplant 
outcomes with chemotherapy-only based conditioning with 
busulfan (Bu) and cyclophosphamide (Cy), compared to 
total body irradiation (TBI) and Cy [5]. Attempts at modify-
ing the conditioning regimen to further improve post-HSCT 
survival include the addition of chemotherapy such as mel-
phalan to minimize leukemic burden prior to HSCT [6], and 
replacement of Cy with agents such as fludarabine (Flu) and 
clofarabine to decrease transplant-related toxicity [6,7].
At our institution, AML patients who received HSCT in 
second CR previously received conditioning with myeloab-
lative Bu/Flu. Since 2014, we amended the regimen to incor-
porate 8 Gy TBI and lower the cumulative Bu dose (TBI/Bu/
Flu) with the aim of decreasing relapse with a relatively low-
er dose of TBI, while also decreasing non-relapse mortality 
(NRM) through an overall lower dose of Bu. In this study, we 
retrospectively analyzed the outcome and prognostic factors

Original Article
Purpose  The optimum conditioning regimen for pediatric acute myeloid leukemia (AML) patients who receive allogeneic hematopoi-
etic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) in second complete remission (CR) remains unclear. In this study, we reviewed the outcome of 
AML patients treated with HSCT in second CR at our institution, focusing on the role of the conditioning regimen on post-transplant 
survival.  
Materials and Methods  We evaluated 43 AML patients who received myeloablative HSCT in second CR at our institution from 2009 
to 2023. Thirteen of the patients (30.2%) received conditioning with busulfan and fludarabine (Bu/Flu), while 30 patients (69.8%) 
were treated with total body irradiation (TBI) 8 Gy combined with Bu and Flu (TBI/Bu/Flu). 
Results  Thirty-two of the patients received a haploidentical family donor (HFD) transplant (74.4%). Estimated 5-year event-free 
survival (EFS) was 57.5%±7.6% (24/43), with 5-year cumulative incidence of relapse and non-relapse mortality of 30.7%±7.2% and 
11.8%±5.0%, respectively. Among risk factors for EFS, only the conditioning regimen was important with patients treated with TBI/
Bu/Flu having significantly higher 5-year EFS than those who received Bu/Flu (73.0%±8.2% vs. 23.1%±11.7%, p=0.004). There was 
no significant difference in 5-year EFS between patients who received HFD transplant and those treated with transplant from other 
donors. Estimated 5-year overall survival was 66.8%±7.3% (28/43), with all 28 surviving patients remaining disease-free at last 
follow-up. 
Conclusion  A myeloablative TBI/Bu/Flu regimen resulted in 5-year EFS of 73.0%±8.2%, indicating a favorable long-term outcome for 
AML patients in second CR who receive HSCT with this conditioning regimen compared to previous studies.

Materials and Methods
1. Patient group
We evaluated AML patients who consecutively received 
allogeneic HSCT in second CR from April 1, 2009 to Decem-
ber 31, 2023 at our institution. We excluded one acute pro-
myelocytic leukemia (APL) patient who received allogeneic 
HSCT in second CR to focus on the outcome of non-APL 
AML patients. We further excluded one patient treated with 
HSCT for relapse and clonal evolution to acute bilineage leu-
kemia after initial treatment for AML, and one patient treat-
ed with a conditioning regimen other than Bu/Flu or TBI/
Bu/Flu in second CR. The final study group consisted of 43 
patients (Table 1). Nineteen of the patients had previously 
been reported as part of different studies [2,8,9]. 
2. Conditioning regimen and graft-versus-host disease 
prophylaxis
 Donor-patient human leukocyte antigen (HLA) typ-
ing was done for HLA-A, -B, -C and -DR at the allele level. 
Patients who received HSCT from 2009 to 2013 were treated 
with a conditioning regimen consisting of intravenous Bu 130 
mg/m2/day (day –6 to day –3), and Flu 40 mg/m2/day (day 
–6 to day –3). In contrast, the conditioning regimen for the 
majority of patients who received HSCT from 2014 onwards 
was the following: fractionated TBI 8 Gy total (day –8 to day 
–7), Bu 130 mg/m2/day (day –6 to day –5), and Flu 40 mg/
m2/day (day –6 to day –3). Bu was given intravenously as a 
single dose each day, and Bu drug levels were not assessed.
Patients treated with unrelated donor (UD) or haploidenti-
cal family donor (HFD) HSCT received in vivo T-cell deple-
tion. HSCT recipients from 2009 to 2018 were treated with 
rabbit anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG) (thymoglobulin) at 
total doses ranging from 3.75 mg/kg to 7.5 mg/kg for UD 
HSCT, and 5 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg for HFD HSCT. ATG was 
combined with cyclosporine and low-dose methotrexate for 
overall graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) prophylaxis. The 
majority of UD or HFD HSCT patients from 2019 onwards 
received post-transplantation cyclophosphamide (PTCy) at 
standard doses of 50 mg/kg/day for 2 days on days 3 and 
4. For these patients, overall GVHD prophylaxis consisted of 
PTCy combined with cyclosporine starting on day 5.
3. Study outcomes and definitions
The main study outcomes were the event-free survival 
(EFS) and overall survival (OS) of the study group of patients 
who received HSCT in second CR. We analyzed the follow-
ing variables with regard to prognostic impact on EFS in 
univariate analysis: sex, age at diagnosis, genetic risk group, 
overall AML risk group, history of previous HSCT, time to 
relapse, number of pre-transplant chemotherapy courses 
after relapse, period of HSCT, donor type, conditioning 
regimen, GVHD prophylaxis, diagnosis of acute or chronic 
GVHD. Prognostic variables with p < 0.1 regarding EFS were 
evaluated in the multivariate setting.
We also determined the incidence of relapse, NRM, acute 
GVHD (both grades 2-4 and grades 3-4), and chronic GVHD 
(overall, as well as moderate/severe). Acute and chronic 
GVHD were graded according to established criteria [10,11].
The date of neutrophil engraftment was defined as the first 
of three consecutive days with absolute neutrophil count 
> 0.5×109/L and the date of platelet engraftment was defined 
as the first of three consecutive days with platelet count 
> 20×109/L without platelet transfusions in the preceding 
week.
4. Statistics
Comparison of categorical and continuous variables bet-
ween Bu/Flu and TBI/Bu/Flu subgroups was done with 
Fisher’s exact test and Mann-Whitney U test, respectively. 
The EFS of the patients was determined from the time of 
HSCT to relapse, death in CR, diagnosis of secondary malig-
nancy, or last follow-up, while OS was calculated from the 
time of HSCT to death or last follow-up. EFS and OS were 
estimated with the Kaplan-Meier method. Univariate and 
multivariate analysis of risk factors for EFS were done with 
the log-rank test and Cox proportional hazards regression, 
respectively. Both acute GVHD and chronic GVHD were 
considered time-dependent covariates when analyzing their 
impact on EFS.
Incidence of relapse, NRM, acute and chronic GVHD were 
calculated with the cumulative incidence (CI) function, and 
comparison according to groups was done with Gray’s test. 
Competing risks were death in CR for relapse, AML relapse 
for NRM, relapse or NRM without acute GVHD within 
100 days post-HSCT for acute GVHD, and relapse or NRM 
without chronic GVHD within 1 year post-HSCT for chronic 
GVHD.
p-values < 0.05 were considered significant. Patient follow-
up was done up to December 31, 2025.

6. Overall survival
The estimated 5-year OS was 66.8%±7.3% (28/43), with all 
28 surviving patients remaining disease-free at last follow-
up (Fig. 2). Of the 19 patients who experienced an event, 3 
patients who relapsed post-HSCT achieved CR once more 
and remain disease-free (chemotherapy only, chemotherapy 
and donor lymphocyte infusion, chemotherapy and second 
HSCT, respectively), and one patient diagnosed with thyroid 
cancer as a second malignancy underwent curative surgery.
Discussion
Recent studies have shown long-term OS of 30%-40% for 
relapsed pediatric AML patients [12,13]. In contrast, patients 
who receive allogeneic HSCT in second CR have shown 
5-year LFS of 40%-50% [3,4], indicating improved outcome 
for relapsed patients who are able to achieve second CR and 
proceed to HSCT. The 5-year EFS of 57.5% for our study 
group was comparable to the survival rates reported in these 
past studies.
The conditioning regimen may have a significant impact 
on the outcome of pediatric AML patients who receive HSCT. 
One key comparison is Bu-based chemotherapy only condi-
tioning versus the incorporation of TBI as part of the condi-
tioning regimen. Past studies in pediatric AML patients have 
either shown no difference in major transplant outcomes 
between the two conditioning types [14,15], or improved 
survival with Bu-based chemotherapy conditioning in first 
CR [16]. Modification of the standard Bu/Cy has also been 
attempted, with one study reporting higher 5-year LFS with 
the addition of melphalan (BuCyMel), or the replacement of 
Cy with clofarabine and fludarabine (CloFluBu), compared 
to BuCy [6].
In our study, we found that the conditioning regimen 
was the only significant factor for EFS, with patients who 
received a TBI/Bu/Flu regimen having higher EFS than 
those treated with Bu/Flu. The significantly lower relapse 
rate in the patients who received TBI/Bu/Flu compared 
with those treated with Bu/Flu contributed to the higher 
EFS in these patients. An early study added 4 Gy TBI to mye-
loablative Bu/Flu to limit potential increase in relapse rate 
resulting from concurrent use of ATG in the study protocol 
[17]. The study found similarly high disease-free survival for 
both matched related donor and alternative donor HSCTs in 
adult acute leukemia patients. Subsequent studies of 4 Gy 
TBI/Bu/Flu conditioning focused on adult AML patients 
showed conflicting results on whether the addition of TBI 
improved OS compared to Bu/Flu alone [18,19]. In addition, 
a study of adult AML patients showed that HFD transplant 
based on a higher dose of 8 Gy TBI combined with Bu/Flu 
and ATG resulted in outcome comparable to matched UD 
(MUD) transplant [20]. While previous studies of TBI/Bu/
Flu were mostly based on patients who received HSCT in

in patients treated with TBI/Bu/Flu in second CR, empha-

significant predictors of outcome in our second CR HSCT

nostic factors in relapsed AML. The majority of our patients 
also received HSCT from an HFD (n=32, 74.4%), indicating 
a strategy to consolidate second CR with allogeneic HSCT, 
regardless of the presence of a well-matched donor. We found

We emphasize the major limitations of our study. Patients 
in the earlier part of our study period received Bu/Flu con-
ditioning, while most of the patients in the later period were 
treated with TBI/Bu/Flu. Although supportive measures 
remained largely unchanged throughout this period, changes 
in GVHD prophylaxis methods and improvements in over-
all transplant procedures may have contributed to the better 
outcome of TBI/Bu/Flu recipients, as manifest in the lower 
NRM for these patients. Multivariate analysis of the impact 
of both conditioning regimen and transplant period on EFS 
did not show a significant factor for outcome, underscoring 
the limitation of concluding upon conditioning regimen as a 
key prognostic factor, independent of transplant period.
Although the univariate study showed significantly higher 
EFS for TBI/Bu/Flu compared to Bu/Flu, our small patient 
group data derive from a single institution, and further study 
is necessary to conclude upon the relative advantages of 
TBI/Bu/Flu over Bu-based regimens which are commonly 
used in the transplant of pediatric AML patients. Our main 
aim in this paper is to emphasize the favorable outcomes of 
our patients treated with TBI/Bu/Flu, rather than to con-
clude upon its relative efficacy compared to other condition-
ing regimens.
Utilization of TBI as part of the conditioning regimen 
allowed for a decrease in the overall Bu dose, and may have 
contributed to lower alkylating agent-related toxicities. How-
ever, TBI incorporation in the transplant of pediatric AML 
patients may result in a higher incidence of late effects. One 
registry study of 624 children with AML found a decreased 
relapse incidence, but higher NRM in patients treated with 
TBI as part of the conditioning regimen, resulting in similar 
LFS and OS between the TBI and non-TBI groups. However, 
patients who received TBI showed a significantly higher 
3-year incidence of endocrine problems such as gonadal and 
growth hormone deficiency [22]. In contrast, another large-
scale study focused specifically on adolescent and young 
adult AML patients reported that the incidence of late effects 
depended more on the development of chronic GVHD rather 
than the use of TBI in conditioning [23].
In summary, we found that pediatric AML patients in sec-
ond CR treated with a myeloablative TBI/Bu/Flu condition-
ing regimen achieved a high 5-year EFS of 73.0%. HSCT with 
TBI/Bu/Flu conditioning may result in favorable long-term 
outcome for relapsed AML patients who are able to achieve 
second CR.
