Abstract
Weighted-indexed semi-Markov chains (WISMCs) enrich a semi-Markov state process with an
endogenous index that summarises the past trajectory through exponentially weighted mem-
ory. Existing applications mainly treat recurrent systems and usually choose the score function
deﬁning the index by modelling intuition and speciﬁc knowledge of the studied system. We de-
velop the absorbing case, in which the transient dynamics end through one of several competing
terminal causes, and we separate the theory conditional on a ﬁxed score from the speciﬁcation
problem of choosing that score. We deﬁne an absorbing WISMC on the augmented state-index
space, discuss the aﬃne normalisation of the index score and the stability of the model with
respect to it, derive indexed renewal equations for cause-speciﬁc absorption probabilities, ﬁrst-
passage distributions and survival, and show that, under a uniform absorption condition, the
ﬁxed-point solutions are unique and the iteration converges geometrically. The framework is mo-
tivated by credit default/prepayment, reliability failure modes, and energy-community shortage
events; a synthetic numerical illustration shows how the theoretical quantities are computed.
Keywords: weighted-indexed semi-Markov chain; absorbing state; competing risks; Markov re-
newal equation; ﬁrst-passage time; index score.

Introduction
Weighted-Indexed Semi-Markov Chains (WISMCs) model systems whose transition law depends
both on the present state and on an endogenous index that summarises the past trajectory through
exponentially weighted memory (D’Amico, 2011; D’Amico and Petroni, 2011, 2012, 2018, 2021;
De Blasis, 2023; D’Amico et al., 2024a,b). Two systems in the same state may have reached it
through diﬀerent histories, and these histories may aﬀect both the next transition and the next
sojourn time; the index retains this information without enlarging the state to the whole past.
WISMCs have been applied in ﬁnance, energy and reliability (D’Amico et al., 2015a), building on
the classical Markov renewal and semi-Markov theory (Pyke, 1961a,b; Çinlar, 1975; Limnios and
Oprişan, 2001; Janssen and Manca, 2006; Barbu and Limnios, 2008). Second-order semi-Markov
chains, analysed by D’Amico et al. (2015b) with an application to wind energy production, provide
a related way of introducing memory into the kernel.
Existing WISMC applications mainly concern recurrent dynamics. Many systems, however,
are terminal: a loan ends in default or prepayment, a component fails through one of several
modes, an energy system enters a shortage condition. The relevant quantities are then ﬁrst-passage

times, cause-speciﬁc absorption probabilities and survival, as in the competing-risk and multi-
state literature (Aalen and Johansen, 1978; Fine and Gray, 1999; Hougaard, 2000; Kalbﬂeisch and
Prentice, 2002; Putter et al., 2007; Geskus, 2024). Default and prepayment are standard competing
exits in credit and mortgage modelling (Deng et al., 2000; Chamboko and Bravo, 2020; Saavedra
et al., 2024), and the valuation of mortgages subject to both full prepayment and default risks is
studied by Zhou et al. (2024), and rating dynamics show duration eﬀects and path dependence
that the current rating does not capture (D’Amico et al., 2006, 2010; Lando and Skødeberg, 2002;
Christensen et al., 2004; Frydman and Schuermann, 2008). The rate of occurrence of failures of
Markov processes has also been applied to ﬁnancial credit ratings (D’Amico, 2015). Semi-Markov
and phase-type models with one absorbing state per cause are available in reliability (Wu et al.,
2019; Garcia-Maya et al., 2022; Lindqvist, 2023), but, to our knowledge, their kernels do not depend
on an endogenous memory of the trajectory.
Competing risks have also been modelled within
multi-state system methodology with sojourn times from a general family closed under minima
(Dimitrakopoulou et al., 2025).
This paper develops the absorbing counterpart of the WISMC. We consider a ﬁnite set of
transient states, a compact index space and a ﬁnite set of absorbing causes. Conditional on the
current state and index, an indexed semi-Markov kernel governs the next destination and sojourn;
the index is updated along transient jumps and stopped at absorption.
The process is then a
Markov renewal process on the augmented state-index space, killed at the ﬁrst jump into a cause,
and the post-jump index becomes an argument of every future absorption quantity.
Our ﬁrst
contribution is the resulting calculus: indexed renewal equations for cause-speciﬁc ﬁrst-passage
distributions, survival and ultimate cause probabilities, with minimality of the solutions and, under
a uniform absorption condition, uniqueness and geometric convergence of the ﬁxed-point iteration.
The ﬁrst-step arguments are standard; what is new is that the terminal components of the kernel
determine the cause-speciﬁc laws, while the transient components propagate the index into all
future quantities.
Our second contribution concerns the index itself. The index depends not only on the memory
parameter but also on the score that maps the trajectory into the remembered quantity, which
previous applications ﬁx by modelling intuition and speciﬁc knowledge of the studied system. In
an absorbing model this choice directly aﬀects the absorption probabilities. We show that the score
is identiﬁed only up to aﬃne transformations, introduce normalised admissible classes, prove that
the index and the kernel are Lipschitz in the score, and give conditions under which the absorption
probabilities are Lipschitz in the score as well. Comparing candidate scores is therefore meaningful,
although a selection procedure is not developed here. A synthetic credit-risk illustration compares
the ﬁxed-point and renewal-equation solutions with Monte Carlo estimates and shows the eﬀect of
the index and of the score on the cause-speciﬁc probabilities.
The construction diﬀers from three standard alternatives. Duration-dependent semi-Markov
kernels condition on backward time but do not propagate a memory built from the pre-entry path;
multi-state and cause-speciﬁc hazard models treat history through covariates or frailties rather
than through a recursively updated index; macro-factor migration models condition on external
stress, whereas the WISMC index is an internal compression of the observed trajectory. The model
is thus most useful when past stress must keep aﬀecting absorption after the observed state has
changed. Reward functionals accumulated until absorption, such as discounted losses and their
decomposition by cause, are not treated here and are left to separate work. For reward processes of
semi-Markov type see, for example, Silvestrov and Manca (2017) and D’Amico and Vergine (2026).
Section 2 deﬁnes the absorbing WISMC and Section 3 studies the index score. Section 4 derives
the absorption equations and Section 5 their stability with respect to the score. Section 6 presents
the illustration and Section 7 concludes.

The index is observed at jump times, but its canonical WISMC update is a calendar-time
update; however, through an appropriate choice of the score function Λ, the memory mechanism
can be constructed so that the inﬂuence of past observations decays as the duration of the most
recent sojourn increases. During the sojourn [Tn, Tn+1) the kernel uses the pre-jump value Un; after
a transient jump, the new value is obtained by applying Λ with the realised holding time Sn+1. This
convention preserves the original WISMC idea that long sojourns should have a diﬀerent memory
eﬀect from short sojourns.
The object driving all later equations is therefore the family of indexed kernels
{Qij(u; ·) : i ∈E◦, j ∈E, u ∈U}.
The competing-risk structure is carried by the terminal components Qia(u; ·), a ∈A, while the
path dependence is carried by the deterministic update of Un. This separation is important: once
the score and update rule deﬁning Un are ﬁxed, the model is an Markov renewal process on the
augmented space E◦× U, killed when the next destination lies in A.
3
The index-score speciﬁcation problem
In the WISMC framework, the index is deﬁned through a map Λ, whose speciﬁcation typically
involves both a memory parameter, regulating the persistence of past information, and a score
function f, determining which features of the trajectory are accumulated in the index. In earlier
applications, this score is often ﬁxed heuristically and tailored to the phenomenon under study.
For absorbing competing-risk models, however, this choice has direct consequences for the resulting
terminal cause probabilities.

7
Conclusion
This paper introduced an absorbing version of weighted-indexed semi-Markov chains with com-
peting terminal causes. The construction preserves the main advantage of WISMCs, namely a
tractable endogenous memory of the past trajectory, while extending the model to systems that are
naturally killed by terminal events. Once the process is viewed as a Markov renewal chain on the
augmented transient state-index space, the classical semi-Markov renewal calculus can be rewrit-
ten in a form that keeps track of the post-jump index. This leads to cause-speciﬁc ﬁrst-passage
equations and ultimate absorption probabilities in which the future law depends not only on the
current state but also on the accumulated history encoded by the index.
A central point of the paper is that this index is not fully speciﬁed by the memory parameter.
The score function used to build the index aﬀects terminal probabilities, and therefore it should
not be treated as an arbitrary heuristic.
The aﬃne normalisation shows which features of the
score are identiﬁable, and the stability results give conditions under which terminal probabilities
depend Lipschitz-continuously on it. Together they make a predictive comparison of candidate
scores within an admissible class meaningful.

The synthetic credit-risk illustration gives a controlled example of the mechanism. In the con-
trolled kernel, a high stress index raises downgrade and default probabilities, and, by construction
of the update, its eﬀect fades gradually as the trajectory returns to good states. The example shows
how two obligors in the same current state may have diﬀerent terminal probabilities because they
have diﬀerent histories. It also veriﬁes numerically that the cause-speciﬁc ﬁxed-point and renewal
equations can be solved directly and agree with Monte Carlo estimates for the controlled kernel.
Several extensions remain open. The substochastic pre-absorption dynamics could be devel-
oped into a quasi-stationary theory under state- and index-dependent survival. Quasi-stationary
asymptotics for perturbed semi-Markov processes in discrete time (Petersson, 2017) are a natural
reference point for this extension. Another natural direction is to develop the likelihood theory un-
der right censoring and delayed entry, where the observational start time need not coincide with the
process origin. A third is to allow non-homogeneous calendar-time kernels, so that macroeconomic
conditions aﬀect the transition law together with the endogenous index. Further developments
include portfolio interactions, stochastic marks attached to terminal jumps, semi-parametric or
RKHS score selection, and reward functionals accumulated until absorption, such as discounted
losses, their higher moments and their decomposition by cause. These extensions would preserve
the same core idea developed here: absorbing competing-risk dynamics can be studied through a
semi-Markov renewal structure once the relevant memory has been encoded in a well-deﬁned index.
The numerical illustration is synthetic and is intended as a controlled veriﬁcation of the equa-
tions, not as an empirical credit-risk calibration; in particular, the implied default probabilities are
much higher than those of real loan portfolios. Estimation is not studied, and no ﬁnite-sample
theory is given. The non-indexed semi-Markov benchmark in the illustration is calibrated on sim-
ulated paths and should be read as a baseline for the controlled experiment, not as evidence from
observed data.
